# Marathi ASR: `marathi_lrec2020`

Recipe for Marathi ASR on the crowdsourced Marathi speech data of Abraham et al., *Crowdsourcing Speech Data for Low-Resource Languages from Low-Income Workers* (LREC 2020). The data is downloaded from the [IIT Bombay IndicCorpora page](https://www.cse.iitb.ac.in/~pjyothi/indiccorpora/#marathi).

`run.sh` runs the whole recipe: data preparation, LM and ASR training, then decoding of `marathi_dev` and `marathi_test` with and without the LM.

- ASR training: **`conf/train.yaml`** (character Conformer: 3 encoder blocks, 256-dim, 3-block Transformer decoder; `batch_bins: 16000000`, `accum_grad: 4`, Adam `lr: 0.0005`, warmup 5000 steps, 30 epochs, SpecAugment, hybrid CTC/attention `ctc_weight: 0.3`, label smoothing `lsm_weight: 0.1`). Training data is speed-perturbed (0.9, 1.0, 1.1), and utterances longer than 12 s are removed from the training and validation data (`--max_wav_duration 12`). The ASR model used for decoding, `valid.acc.ave.pth`, is the average of the 10 checkpoints with the best validation accuracy.
- LM training: **`conf/train_lm.yaml`** (character LSTM LM) on the training transcripts `data/marathi_train/text`. Decoding uses `valid.loss.ave.pth`, the average of the 5 checkpoints with the lowest validation loss.
- Decoding with the LM: **`conf/decode.yaml`** (beam 20, `ctc_weight: 0.5`, `lm_weight: 0.3`).
- Decoding without the LM: **`conf/tuning/decode_no_lm.yaml`** (the same with `lm_weight: 0.0`).

The recordings are `.3gp` files, which `wav.scp` decodes with **ffmpeg**, so ffmpeg must be on `PATH` (`local/data.sh` checks for it).

The trained ASR model and LM are available at [espnet/marathi_lrec2020](https://huggingface.co/espnet/marathi_lrec2020) on Hugging Face.

The [RESULTS](#results) below were generated with `scripts/utils/show_asr_result.sh exp/asr_train_raw_char_sp`. `marathi_dev` is also the validation set, so its results appear under `org/marathi_dev` (all 9,277 utterances, before the length filter).

## Corpus

- 109 h of read Marathi speech from 36 participants, 12 in each of three groups: rural low-income workers, urban low-income workers and engineering students. The released files carry 31 speaker IDs (College-Students 10, Rural-Low-Income-Workers 9, Urban-Low-Income-Workers 12).
- 3,000 sentences taken from children's stories on Pratham StoryWeaver, recorded on Android phones (`.3gp`, AMR-NB, 8 kHz, mono). Each participant read the same sentences.
- Download `Marathi.tgz` from the [IIT Bombay IndicCorpora page](https://www.cse.iitb.ac.in/~pjyothi/indiccorpora/#marathi). It is a plain tar file that holds one gzipped archive per group:

  ```sh
  tar xf Marathi.tgz
  cd Marathi
  for g in College-Students Rural-Low-Income-Workers Urban-Low-Income-Workers; do tar xzf ${g}.tgz; done
  ```

  Then set `MARATHI_LREC2020` in `db.sh` to this `Marathi` directory.
- Licence: the paper states that the data "is released to the public, free of cost". Neither the paper nor the download page gives a licence. Please cite the paper below if you use the data.

## Data split

The train/dev/test split is a random 80/10/10 split **by sentence ID** (seed 0) generated in `local/data_prep.py`, not an official split, so the results cannot be compared with prior work.

| set | sentences | utterances | speakers |
|-----|----------:|-----------:|---------:|
| `marathi_train` | 2,393 | 73,893 | 31 |
| `marathi_dev`   |   299 |  9,277 | 31 |
| `marathi_test`  |   300 |  9,301 | 31 |

No sentence appears in more than one set, but all speakers appear in all three, so the test set measures unseen sentences from seen speakers.

## Difference from `mr_openslr64`

`egs2/mr_openslr64` uses OpenSLR SLR64 (He et al., LREC 2020): 3 h of studio-quality speech from 9 female speakers (1,569 sentences), recorded for speech synthesis and released under CC BY-SA 4.0. This corpus is about 36 times larger. It consists of 8 kHz phone recordings made by men and women outside a studio, including low-income workers, most of whom had never used a smartphone before.
